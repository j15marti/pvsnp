                                                  Abstract
          We strengthen a constructive satisfiability framework based on simultaneous forest partitions,
     exact activation signals, local matching, and algebraic interfaces. For proper monotone 3-SAT
     with exactly two occurrences of each polarity per variable, every crossing graph is the line
     graph of an explicitly constructed subcubic multigraph. Minimum crossing covers are therefore
     acquired exactly in polynomial time by maximum matching. This yields a query-specific local
     extension algorithm and improves the combined signal terminal to 2τmax +6k poly( N ), for an integer
     fractional-guard budget k. We also strengthen the representation-specific conservation inequality
     to ρsignal + 7τΣ ≥ n/3.
          For independent private modules coupled by a binary linear equation, we prove an exact
     dynamic program whose states lie in intersections of internal and external linear spans. Its
     aggregate cost permits polynomially many local exponential computations without multiplying
     all local choices together. An infinite family of strictly rank-expanding punctured-plane systems
     has interface width at most two and is solved by this method. We integrate a recently developed
     exact solver for 2-CNF plus bounded-rank affine constraints, and give a strict polynomial-potential
     criterion for cooperative recursive work.
         These results prove additional restricted algorithms and a conditional implication to P = NP.
     The universal theorem needed to acquire an affordable interface or productive contraction on
     every relevant residual remains unproved. Accordingly, this manuscript does not establish an
     unconditional resolution of P versus NP.

Keywords. Satisfiability; exact interfaces; line graphs; matching; affine constraints; branch decompo-
sitions; aggregate complexity.

   The decision problem, the representation, and the computational charge must be kept separate.
We use the following source throughout the forest and signal sections.

Definition 1.1 (Balanced monotone source). A balanced source is a CNF formula F in which every
clause contains three distinct variables, every clause is either entirely positive or entirely negative, no
clause is duplicated, and each variable occurs exactly twice positively and twice negatively. If n is the
number of variables and m the number of clauses, then m = 4n/3. The encoded input length is N.

    This class is NP-complete [3]. The notation (2, 2) specifies occurrence counts. It does not mean
an exact-two-of-four constraint. Recent reports using the latter source [4] can contribute source-
independent lemmas, but their geometric promises and source-specific reductions do not automati-
cally apply to Definition 1.1.
   All algorithms are deterministic and uniform in an ordinary bit model. Every use of poly( N )
denotes a fixed polynomial whose exponent is independent of the input and of the varying structural
parameters. The charge includes parsing, representation conversion, candidate acquisition, failed
probes, exact arithmetic, every recursive child actually executed, stored tables, witness reconstruction,
and final source verification. A supplied decomposition is explicitly identified as supplied; its
verification does not imply efficient discovery.

Definition 1.2 (Authenticated transformation). An authenticated transformation records a precise
relation between a parent constraint system and its successors, together with an effective witness map.
A decision-preserving restriction retains at least one witness whenever the parent is satisfiable. An
exact boundary projection preserves every extendible boundary assignment. An AND decomposition
requires jointly compatible witnesses for all children; an OR decomposition requires a witness for at
least one child. The compatibility operation and the cost of evaluating it are part of the transformation.

   The principal new results are Theorems 4.1, 6.3, 7.1, and 8.1. The conditional endpoint is Theo-
rem 11.3. The latter is deliberately stated with its unresolved hypothesis visible. None of the finite
audits in Section 12 is used as a substitute for a universal theorem.


2    Simultaneous forests and their precise scope

Form a positive clause multigraph G+ whose vertices are positive clauses. Each source variable is an
edge joining its two positive occurrences. Define G− analogously. The two graphs have a common
ground set E, namely the source variables. They are loopless and cubic. Three parallel edges would
give identical ternary clauses, so they do not occur.

Lemma 2.1 (The two graphic rank bounds). For every S ⊆ E,

                                      |S| ≤ 2r+ (S),       |S| ≤ 2r− (S),

where r± are the graphic matroid ranks.

Proof. Consider a connected component of either graph induced by S, with v incident vertices and e
edges. If v = 2, then e ≤ 2 = 2(v − 1). If v = 3, maximum degree three gives e ≤ 4 = 2(v − 1). If
v ≥ 4, then e ≤ 3v/2 ≤ 2(v − 1). There are no loop components. Summing e ≤ 2(v − 1) over the
nontrivial components proves the claim.

Theorem 2.2 (Polynomial simultaneous forest acquisition). Every balanced source admits a partition
E = E+ ∪˙ E− such that E+ is a forest in G+ and E− is a forest in G− . Such a partition is constructible in
deterministic polynomial time.

Proof. Lemma 2.1 implies
                                      |S| ≤ r+ (S) + r− (S)     ( S ⊆ E ).
The matroid union criterion gives a partition into independent sets of the two graphic matroids, and
its augmenting algorithm constructs the partition in polynomial time [5].

    Assign preference pv = 0 to variables of E+ and pv = 1 to variables of E− . Under yv = xv ⊕ pv ,
an occurrence that is positive in y is called an owner occurrence. Thus each variable owns two source
clauses. The owner incidence graphs are subdivisions of the acquired forests. A co-owner block is a
connected component obtained by joining variables that own the same clause; isolated variables are
included.

Proposition 2.3 (Hereditary forest certificates). A simultaneous forest certificate remains valid after
variable pinning and clause deletion, when removed occurrence endpoints are replaced by distinct private leaves
and surviving edges retain their colors.

Proof. Pinning a variable deletes its edge from both occurrence graphs. Deleting a clause splits its
vertex into separate degree-one endpoints for the remaining incident edges. Edge deletion and vertex
splitting cannot create a cycle in a previously acyclic edge set. Unsatisfied clauses shortened by a pin
retain their surviving incidences. Therefore the retained color classes remain forests.

   This proposition applies to a monotone core retained with occurrence identities. Arbitrary
substitution or elimination can create new clauses and new incidences; those operations require
new certificates. The balanced identity m = 4n/3 likewise belongs to the original source and is not
inherited by arbitrary residuals.

Proposition 2.4 (A supplied target is cheaply reachable). Given two valid partitions into independent
sets of two matroids, one can move from the first to the second through at most one vacant element at a time,
using at most 2d opening, exchange, and closing events and exactly d permanent color corrections, where d is
the initial Hamming distance.

Proof. Remove a miscolored element to create a vacancy. Try to insert the vacant element in its target
color. If insertion is independent, close the vacancy. Otherwise the fundamental circuit contains
an element currently in that color but not assigned that color by the target: if all circuit elements
belonged to the target class, that target class would be dependent. Exchange such an element for
the vacant one. The inserted element is now correctly colored and never needs to move again; the
removed element becomes the vacancy. Every exchange or successful closing corrects one element.
There are d corrections and at most d openings.

    The proposition is a target-directed reconfiguration statement. It neither discovers a favorable
target nor bounds the number of targets that might need to be examined.

Theorem 4.1 (The crossing graph is a line graph). There is an explicitly constructible loopless multigraph
HB of maximum degree at most three such that

                                      JB = L ( HB ),     τB = | DB | − ν( HB ),

where ν( HB ) is the maximum matching cardinality. An optimal crossing cover is acquired in polynomial time.

Proof. Every variable belongs to at most two distinct supports in S B , because it has only two negative

two supports S, T becomes an edge ev = aS a T . If it belongs to one support S, create a private dummy
dv and the edge ev = aS dv . Distinct variables may create parallel edges, but no loop is created. The
degree of aS is |S| ≤ 3, and dummy vertices have degree one.

JB = L( HB ). Independent sets in a line graph are precisely matchings in its root multigraph. The
complement of a maximum independent set is a minimum vertex cover. General maximum matching
is polynomial-time computable [6]; parallel edges may be represented by one edge for cardinality
computation while retaining a chosen source label.

Theorem 4.2 (Query-specific local extension). For a partial signal query Q, let JB ( Q) use only the

8.2   A second terminal from incidence width

For a system containing binary clauses and affine rows, form a graph with actual variable vertices,
one vertex for each affine row, an edge for every variable-row incidence, and an edge between the
variables of each binary clause. A long parity row is not expanded into a clique.

Theorem 8.3 (Mixed incidence dynamic programming). A supplied width-w tree decomposition of this
graph yields exact feasibility and witness recovery in 2O(w) poly( N ) time and space. A width O(log N )
promise can be recognized sufficiently for this terminal by deterministic single-exponential treewidth approxi-
mation.

Proof. Use a nice tree decomposition and assign each incidence edge and binary clause to one
processing event. A state stores the Boolean values of live variable vertices and one partial XOR
for each live affine-row vertex. At an incidence event, add that variable’s contribution to the row’s

accumulator. Check a binary clause when its variables are simultaneously present. When a row is
forgotten, require its completed XOR to equal its right-hand side.
    At a join, variable assignments must agree and partial XORs combine by addition over F2 . Each
incidence is processed exactly once, so there is no double counting. Back pointers retain witnesses.
There are at most 2w+1 states in a bag and at most 2O(w) candidate combinations at a join.

instances enter it.

    The parity-state method is consistent with the broader single-exponential incidence-width compi-

affine row bases can have different incidence graphs, so basis selection remains a charged operation.

Proposition 8.4 (Arbitrary mixing remains NP-complete). Conjunctions of 2-CNF clauses and affine
equations over F2 are NP-complete, even when every affine equation has arity three.

Proof. Membership in NP is immediate. A positive exact-one-in-three constraint is exactly

                    x+y+z = 1         (mod 2),      (¬ x ∨ ¬y) ∧ (¬ x ∨ ¬z) ∧ (¬y ∨ ¬z).

Odd parity permits weights one and three, and the binary clauses exclude weights at least two. This
gives a linear reduction from positive exact-one-in-three SAT, an NP-complete language [13].

   Proposition 8.4 is not a proof that the mixed system has no polynomial algorithm. It proves
that separately tractable factor types do not, by themselves, supply such an algorithm. A joint rank,
width, or other exact compatibility theorem is still required.

9.1      Exact two-child gluing and its remaining cost

For one punctured observation pair a( x ) + s a , b( x ) + sb , its nonzero requirement is the union of the

                               a( x ) = 1 + s a or b( x ) = 1 + sb .
Accordingly the full feasible relation is the union of its two restricted child relations. This identity

classifications.

relevant states and the acquisition of their equivalences are bounded. Equation (15), or the explicit
aggregate sum in (12), provides a sufficient resource certificate when its hypotheses are proved.


9.2      Contextual absorption

A private module M(U, B) with exterior E( B, Z ) may be replaced by M′ (U ′ , B) if their projected
boundary relations agree on every boundary value allowed by a certified necessary exterior condition
L( B), where E ⇒ L. A direct proof replaces the private witness while keeping the boundary and
exterior fixed.
    The condition L must be obtained without circularly assuming the module response being
removed. Exact 2-CNF plus affine charts can test relevant absorption questions using Theorem 8.1 at
fixed rank, or Theorem 8.3 at logarithmic incidence width. An arbitrary affine/binary exterior is not
automatically affordable, as Proposition 8.4 demonstrates.

10     What transfers from algebraic complexity and identity testing

The public Anthropic repository claims a separation of algebraic complexity classes and explicitly
states that it does not settle P versus NP [16]. OpenAI’s scope documentation distinguishes exact
formalized arithmetic statements from stronger quantitative statements in its accompanying papers
[17]. We inspected those distinctions and relevant source definitions; we did not independently run
or validate either formal proof development. No claimed external separation is a premise of any
theorem in this manuscript.
    The useful methodological transfer is to specify the external computational problem, the internal
formal representation, and both directions of their correspondence. It also requires charging table
sizes and conversion overhead, and keeping exact polynomial computation separate from Boolean
support testing.

Proposition 10.1 (Ordinary identity testing is not the missing SAT algorithm). A small arithmetic
circuit that agrees with a Boolean formula on Boolean inputs need not be the zero formal polynomial when the
formula is unsatisfiable.

Proof. The contradictory formula x ∧ ¬ x has the arithmetic representative x (1 − x ). This vanishes at
every Boolean point and is a nonzero formal polynomial over every field.

   Reduction modulo xi2 − xi corrects the semantic mismatch, but a polynomially affordable exact
normal form or identity test in that quotient would itself require proof. Likewise, define

                                        GF ( X ) =      ∑        ∏ Xi .
                                                     a∈Sol( F ) i:ai =1

Distinct assignments give distinct squarefree monomials, so GF ̸= 0 over every field exactly when
F is satisfiable. Its definition does not provide a polynomial-size, uniformly constructible circuit.
An exact circuit construction and an adequate deterministic identity test would both have to be
established. Counting models modulo two is different again: a nonempty even-sized solution set can
have zero parity.
   Möbius inversion on an explicit Boolean response table is valid and can compress particular
families of functions. It does not remove the cost of acquiring all required table entries. Recent finite
response compression [18] is therefore useful only with a proved response-acquisition bound and an
exact treatment of infeasible boundary words.


11     The conditional theorem and the unresolved universal step

We now state the strongest justified route from the proved terminals to the requested complexity
conclusion. The statement is a sufficient condition, not a claim that its hypothesis has been established.

Definition 11.1 (Complete cooperative configuration). A configuration records all pending guarded
obligations, exact AND/OR dependencies, compatibility conditions, known tables, witness maps,
and cached results for one original source. It is feasible if its retained data admit at least one complete
original source witness. A transition is sound if every accepted complete witness lifts to a source
witness, and feasibility preserving if a feasible configuration remains feasible until a witness is
returned. Encoded size and the cost of all active tracks are charged jointly. The initial configuration is

Exact interfaces for satisfiability                                                   26 November 2026


the original source formula with no additional restrictions; its identity encoding is constructed in
O( N ) time.
Conjecture 11.2 (Universal affordable acquisition or contraction). There are fixed positive constants
a, b, c, a fixed deterministic procedure, and a nonnegative integer potential Φ on the reachable
complete configurations, with initial value at most ( N + 2) a , having the following properties.


Uniform total work. Each transition, including all discovery attempts, verification, executed termi-
nal work, and retained reconstruction, halts within ( N + 2)b bit operations. All stored representations
and records fit ( N + 2)c bits. These caps apply on satisfiable and unsatisfiable inputs.


Sound transformations. Every accepted transition has authenticated source semantics and pre-
serves feasibility. Compatible witnesses are required at AND nodes; guard identities are retained at
OR nodes. An unresolved terminal may return DECLINE, which does not mean UNSAT.


Universal productive coverage. On every feasible reachable configuration without an available
verified source witness, the procedure either completes witness recovery or acquires an exact terminal
or reduction whose full execution preserves feasibility and decreases Φ by at least one. The acquired
operation may use the signal terminal, algebraic-interface terminal, mixed affine/binary terminal, or
another independently proved terminal. Every child, interface, failed probe, and cooperative track is
charged in the same global configuration.

   One concrete way to establish the potential clause is Theorem 9.1. Another is to acquire a complete
algebraic decomposition whose sum (12) has a fixed polynomial bound. Merely knowing that an
inexpensive terminal exists somewhere is insufficient; the deterministic procedure must acquire a
successful operation.
Theorem 11.3 (Conditional constructive implication). Conjecture 11.2 implies P = NP.

Proof. On a source of Definition 1.1, run the fixed cooperative procedure for a sufficiently large fixed
polynomial clock, for example a constant multiple of

                                           ( N + 2 ) a + b + c +2 ,
which includes initialization, all potential-decreasing transitions, and final witness processing. When-
ever a candidate source assignment appears, check it directly against the original formula.
    On a satisfiable input, the initial configuration is feasible. By authenticated preservation and
universal productive coverage, a feasible unfinished configuration cannot stagnate. At most ( N + 2) a
strict decreases of a nonnegative integer potential are possible. A verified source witness must
therefore be produced within the clock.
    On an unsatisfiable input, no source assignment can pass verification. Return SAT exactly on a
verified assignment and UNSAT if the clock expires without one. The satisfiable-side guarantee just
proved makes the latter decision correct. The procedure is deterministic, total, and polynomial. Since
the balanced source is NP-complete [3], every language in NP has a polynomial-time algorithm.

   The fixed clock in this theorem is justified by the conjecture, not by the present-day behavior of a
heuristic controller. One cannot currently run the available tracks for an arbitrary polynomial time
and declare every timeout unsatisfiable.

11.1    The attempted closure and the exact point where it stops

Theorem 2.2 supplies a forest partition for every source, but does not force τmax + 6ρsignal = O(log N ).
Theorem 4.1 makes crossing-cover acquisition polynomial, but the value of the cover can still grow.
Theorem 6.3 explains why a small global guard budget must coexist with distributed local complexity.
   Theorem 7.1 resolves that distributed complexity when the actual subspace interfaces are afford-
able and the local fiber queries are exact. It does not prove that every compiled system has such a
decomposition. Theorem 8.1 handles an acquired low-rank affine/binary chart, but its exponent
grows with rank. Theorem 8.3 permits logarithmic width with affordable recognition, but there is no
proved universal width or productive-alternative theorem.
    The remaining mathematical obligation is therefore a universal, constructively acquirable disjunc-
tion: every unsolved feasible configuration must have either an affordable exact joint representation
or a sound reduction that decreases a globally charged polynomial potential. Neither a counterex-
ample to one terminal nor a restricted arithmetic lower bound settles that disjunction. Its proof is
necessary before Theorem 11.3 can be promoted to an unconditional P = NP theorem.
